# Mathematical Specification of the MedRAG Method

## 1. Pipeline Overview

MedRAG executes an adaptive 6-phase iterative loop consisting of:
1. Clinical Query Understanding and Risk Classification
2. Hybrid Lexical-Dense Retrieval with Neural Reranking
3. Evidence-Grounded Answer Generation & Claim Decomposition
4. Sentence-Level NLI Verification & Evidence-State Tracking
5. Dynamic Evidence Aggregation with Source Quality Weighting
6. Risk-Conditioned Decision Controller (Stopping, Iteration, or Abstention)

```
User Query x
     │
     ▼
[Phase 1] Query Parsing & Risk Assessment ──► Risk Score ρ_x
     │
     ▼
[Phase 2] Hybrid Retrieval (BM25 + Dense RRF) ──► Cross-Encoder Rerank ──► Context Docs
     │
     ▼
[Phase 3] Evidence-Grounded Generation ──► Atomic Claim Decomposition {c_i}
     │
     ▼
[Phase 4] Multi-Granularity NLI Verification ──► (Entailment, Contradiction, Neutral)
     │
     ▼
[Phase 5] Evidence-State Aggregation ──► Source Reliability & Contradiction State S(c_i)
     │
     ▼
[Phase 6] Risk-Conditioned Decision Policy
     ├── ALL SUPPORTED ───────────────► STOP / VERIFIED
     ├── BUDGET REMAINING + GAPS ─────► SEARCH (Targeted Gap Query q^(t+1) ──► Loop to Phase 2)
     ├── CONFLICT PERSISTS (HIGH RISK) ► ABSTAIN / CONFLICTED
     └── PARTIAL SUPPORT (LOW RISK) ──► PARTIAL (Qualified Disclosure)
```

## 2. Mathematical Definition of Pipeline Components

### Step 1: Query Representation
Given a raw clinical question $x \in \mathcal{X}$, the query understanding module $f_{\text{parse}}$ extracts:

$$q = f_{\text{parse}}(x) = \left\langle x, \mathcal{E}_x, \iota_x, \rho_x, q_{\text{search}} \right\rangle$$

- $\mathcal{E}_x = \{e_1, \dots, e_k\}$: Extracted clinical entities (diseases, drugs, procedures).
- $\iota_x \in \{\text{diagnosis, treatment, prognosis, dosage, safety, mechanism, general}\}$: Intent category.
- $\rho_x \in [0, 1]$: Clinical safety risk score evaluated deterministically via boundary-checked medical risk patterns:
  $$\rho_x = \begin{cases}
  0.85 & \text{if critical risk terms detected (e.g. cardiac arrest, anaphylaxis, overdose, sepsis)} \\
  0.75 & \text{if high risk terms detected (e.g. pregnancy, pediatric, renal/hepatic impairment, anticoagulant)} \\
  0.60 & \text{if moderate risk terms or dosage/safety intent} \\
  0.30 & \text{otherwise (general informational)}
  \end{cases}$$
- $q_{\text{search}}$: Cleaned retrieval query stripped of conversational punctuation.

*Classification*: **Standard practice** for entity and intent extraction; **Newly proposed** is the deterministic clinical risk tiering tied to verification thresholds.

---

### Step 2: Hybrid Retrieval
Let corpus $\mathcal{D} = \{d_1, \dots, d_N\}$ be indexed simultaneously by:
- Sparse lexical BM25 index: $s_{\text{BM25}}(q_{\text{search}}, d)$.
- Dense bi-encoder semantic index: $\phi: \mathcal{T} \to \mathbb{R}^D$ using `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`:
  $$s_{\text{dense}}(q_{\text{search}}, d) = \cos(\phi(q_{\text{search}}), \phi(d)) = \frac{\phi(q_{\text{search}}) \cdot \phi(d)}{\|\phi(q_{\text{search}})\| \|\phi(d)\|}$$

Each retriever returns top-$k_{\text{retrieve}}$ candidates (default $k=20$), denoted as ranked lists $R_{\text{BM25}}$ and $R_{\text{dense}}$.

*Classification*: **Standard technique** in information retrieval.

---

### Step 3: Passage Ranking (RRF & Cross-Encoder)
First-stage fusion merges candidate rankings via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF; Cormack et al., 2009):

$$s_{\text{RRF}}(d) = w_{\text{BM25}} \cdot \frac{1}{k_{\text{RRF}} + r_{\text{BM25}}(d)} + w_{\text{dense}} \cdot \frac{1}{k_{\text{RRF}} + r_{\text{dense}}(d)}$$

where $k_{\text{RRF}} = 60$, $w_{\text{BM25}} = 0.4$, and $w_{\text{dense}} = 0.6$. If document $d$ was not retrieved by retriever $M$, its rank $r_M(d) = \infty$.
Ties are broken deterministically by $\min(r_{\text{BM25}}, r_{\text{dense}})$, then by `doc_id`.

Second-stage neural reranking evaluates top RRF candidates with a cross-encoder model $\psi_{\text{rerank}}$ (`ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`):

$$s_{\text{final}}(d) = \psi_{\text{rerank}}(q_{\text{search}}, d_{\text{text}}[:1000])$$

The top-$k_{\text{rerank}}$ passages form the context set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{ctx}} = [d_{(1)}, \dots, d_{(K)}]$ (default $K=5$).

*Classification*: **Standard technique** (two-stage retrieval pipeline).

---

### Step 4: Claim Decomposition
Candidate answer $y = \mathcal{G}(x, \mathcal{D}_{\text{ctx}})$ is generated by LLM $\mathcal{G}$ with evidence citations.
The claim decomposition operator $\mathcal{T}_{\text{claim}}$ maps $y$ to a set of atomic, self-contained clinical assertions:

$$\mathcal{C}(y) = \mathcal{T}_{\text{claim}}(y) = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_m\}$$

Each claim $c_i$ satisfies:
1. **Atomicity**: Contains a single clinical subject, relation, and predicate (no compound conjunctions).
2. **Falsifiability**: Directly verifiable against literature without implicit anaphora or deictic references.

*Classification*: **Established concept** in factuality research (Min et al., 2023; Chern et al., 2023), specialized here to clinical claims.

---

### Step 5: Claim-to-Evidence Matching
Every context document $d \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{ctx}}$ is decomposed into multi-granularity premises:
- Full document excerpt: $p_{d, 0} = d_{\text{text}}[:1000]$.
- Sentence-level premises: $\{p_{d, j}\}_{j=1}^{n_d}$ where each sentence $s_j$ is augmented with document title context: $p_{d, j} = \text{Title}_d \circ ". " \circ s_j$.

Premise universe: $\mathcal{P} = \bigcup_{d \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{ctx}}} \{p_{d, 0}, p_{d, 1}, \dots, p_{d, n_d}\}$.
Cross-product evaluation forms pairs: $\mathcal{U} = \{(p, c_i) \mid p \in \mathcal{P}, c_i \in \mathcal{C}\}$.

*Classification*: **Newly proposed** multi-granularity premise representation with title-context injection for medical sentences.

---

### Step 6: Entailment and Contradiction Scoring
Each pair $(p, c_i) \in \mathcal{U}$ is scored by cross-encoder NLI model $\psi_{\text{NLI}}$ (`nli-MiniLM2-L6-H768`):

$$\mathbf{z}(p, c_i) = \psi_{\text{NLI}}(p[:1000], c_i[:512]) \in \mathbb{R}^3$$

$$\mathbf{p}(p, c_i) = \text{Softmax}(\mathbf{z}(p, c_i)) = \left[ p_{\text{con}}(p, c_i), \; p_{\text{ent}}(p, c_i), \; p_{\text{neu}}(p, c_i) \right]$$

*Classification*: **Standard technique** (cross-encoder NLI).

---

### Step 7: Evidence-State Aggregation
For each claim $c_i$:
- Support score:
  $$s_{\text{sup}}(c_i) = \max_{p \in \mathcal{P}} p_{\text{ent}}(p, c_i)$$
- Conflict score (evaluated across material contradictions where contradiction dominates):
  $$s_{\text{con}}(c_i) = \max_{p \in \mathcal{E}^-(c_i)} p_{\text{con}}(p, c_i) \quad \text{where} \quad \mathcal{E}^-(c_i) = \{p \in \mathcal{P} \mid p_{\text{con}} \ge \tau_{\text{con}} \wedge p_{\text{con}} > p_{\text{ent}} \wedge p_{\text{con}} > p_{\text{neu}}\}$$
- Source reliability:
  $$w(d) = 0.6 \cdot \sigma(d) + 0.4 \cdot \max(0, 1 - 0.02 \cdot (Y_{\text{ref}} - Y_d))$$
  $$w_{\text{source}}(c_i) = \max_{p \in \mathcal{E}^+(c_i)} w(d(p))$$
- Heuristic clinical confidence:
  $$\mathcal{H}(c_i) = \max\left(0.0, \; \min\left(1.0, \; s_{\text{sup}}(c_i) \cdot w_{\text{source}}(c_i) - 0.5 \cdot s_{\text{con}}(c_i)\right)\right)$$

*Classification*: **Newly proposed** asymmetric contradiction penalty and source reliability integration for claim verification.

---

### Step 8: Risk-Conditioned Decision Policy
The decision controller $\pi(\mathcal{S}, \rho_x, t)$ maps the current evidence state $\mathcal{S} = \{\mathcal{S}(c_1), \dots, \mathcal{S}(c_m)\}$, clinical risk score $\rho_x$, and round $t$ to an action $a \in \mathcal{A}$:

$$\begin{aligned}
\tau_{\text{sup}}(\rho_x) &= \begin{cases} \min(0.95, \; \tau_{\text{sup}} + 0.05) & \text{if } \rho_x \ge \rho_{\text{high}} \\ \tau_{\text{sup}} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \\
\tau_{\text{con}}(\rho_x) &= \begin{cases} \max(0.20, \; \tau_{\text{con}} - 0.10) & \text{if } \rho_x \ge \rho_{\text{high}} \\ \tau_{\text{con}} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \\
\text{AllowPartial}(\rho_x) &= \begin{cases} \text{False} & \text{if } \rho_x \ge \rho_{\text{high}} \\ \text{True} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{aligned}$$

#### Policy Transition Rules:
1. **STOP / VERIFIED**:
   $$\forall c_i \in \mathcal{C}, \quad \text{status}(c_i) = \text{SUPPORTED}$$
2. **SEARCH**:
   $$\exists c_i \in \mathcal{C} \text{ with } \text{status}(c_i) \neq \text{SUPPORTED} \quad \wedge \quad t < T_{\text{max}}$$
3. **CONFLICTED**:
   $$\exists c_i \in \mathcal{C} \text{ with } \text{status}(c_i) = \text{CONFLICTED} \quad \wedge \quad t \ge T_{\text{max}}$$
4. **PARTIAL**:
   $$t \ge T_{\text{max}} \quad \wedge \quad \exists c_i \text{ SUPPORTED} \quad \wedge \quad \forall c_i, s_{\text{con}}(c_i) < \tau_{\text{con}} \quad \wedge \quad \text{AllowPartial}(\rho_x) = \text{True}$$
5. **ABSTAIN**:
   $$t \ge T_{\text{max}} \quad \wedge \quad \left( \forall c_i, \text{status}(c_i) = \text{MISSING} \;\vee\; (\exists c_i \text{ CONFLICTED} \wedge \rho_x \ge \rho_{\text{high}}) \;\vee\; \text{AllowPartial}(\rho_x) = \text{False} \right)$$

*Classification*: **Newly proposed** risk-conditioned decision policy that couples risk tiering to verification thresholds and selective abstention.

---

### Step 9: Iterative Retrieval (Targeted Gap Query)
When $a = \text{SEARCH}$ at round $t < T_{\text{max}}$:
1. Identify evidence gaps: $\mathcal{C}_{\text{weak}} = \{c_i \in \mathcal{C} \mid \text{status}(c_i) \in \{\text{CONFLICTED, MISSING, UNCERTAIN}\}\}$.
2. Formulate targeted search query:
   $$q^{(t+1)} = \text{GapQueryGenerator}(x, \mathcal{C}_{\text{weak}})$$
   Extracts clinical entities, specific drugs, lab cutoffs, and disease terms from the unresolved claims.
3. Retrieve new documents $\mathcal{D}^{(t+1)} \leftarrow \text{Retrieve}(q^{(t+1)})$.
4. Accumulate unique documents: $\mathcal{D}_{\text{accum}}^{(t+1)} = \mathcal{D}_{\text{accum}}^{(t)} \cup \mathcal{D}^{(t+1)}$.
5. Re-verify claims against accumulated premises.

*Classification*: **Newly proposed** claim-specific gap query formulation directed specifically at unresolved NLI premises.

---

### Step 10: Stopping and Abstention
- If action is `STOP`: synthesize final verified answer with citations to $\mathcal{E}^+(c_i)$.
- If action is `PARTIAL`: disclose verified claims with explicit qualification of unresolved evidence.
- If action is `CONFLICTED`: report the conflicting clinical positions without recommending an unverified action.
- If action is `ABSTAIN`: withhold clinical recommendation, outputting reason (e.g. "Insufficient or contradictory evidence under high patient risk").

*Classification*: **Newly proposed** multi-level selective disclosure framework for medical RAG.
